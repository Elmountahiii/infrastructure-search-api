Circular Water Infrastructure Programme

Fictional demonstration document for Baden-Württemberg, published on 1 June 2025 by the Regional Utilities Innovation Board (Fictional).

Strategic aim

The programme reduces demand for treated drinking water by expanding non-potable reuse at industrial estates, municipal depots, and large public landscapes. Projects must protect public health and maintain reliable wastewater treatment during dry weather.

Reclaimed-water network

A new polishing facility at the fictional Grünbach treatment works will supply reclaimed water through a twelve-kilometre purple-pipe network. Initial customers include a paper mill, a bus depot, and the municipal parks service. Storage tanks will balance daily demand and allow the treatment process to stop safely during maintenance.

Stormwater projects

Three redevelopment areas will use permeable paving, tree pits, and underground attenuation cells. Captured rainfall will irrigate street trees and sports fields. Overflow routes will be mapped so that exceptional storms do not direct water toward homes or critical substations.

Monitoring and safeguards

Online sensors will monitor turbidity, conductivity, and disinfectant performance. Automatic valves will isolate supply when readings exceed agreed limits. Operators will publish annual water savings, reuse reliability, energy consumption, and stormwater volume retained.

Programme timetable

Planning and permits continue through 2025. Construction of the polishing facility begins in 2026, the first reuse customers connect in 2027, and the full monitoring report is scheduled for June 2028.
